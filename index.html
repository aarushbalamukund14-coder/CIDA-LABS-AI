Introduction

Many everyday products — shampoo, soap, face wash, hand wash, cream, dishwashing liquid and detergent — contain chemicals that may be acidic, neutral or basic. The pH scale tells us which one a product is, and products with a pH close to human skin are generally safer for regular use.

WELL-BEING

Skin pH

4.5 – 5.5
AVERAGE HEALTHY SKIN pH

Healthy human skin is slightly acidic. This mild acidity helps support the skin barrier and protects the skin from harmful bacteria.

SCIENCE LAB · METHOD

Testing procedure

  1. Label the cups A, B, C, D, E, F and G.
  2. Place a small amount of each product into a separate cup.
  3. Add one tablespoon of water.
  4. Mix gently.
  1. Dip the strip into the sample.
  2. Wait 2–3 seconds.
  3. Compare the colour with the chart.
  4. Record the pH value.
  1. Chop red cabbage leaves.
  2. Soak them in hot water for 15 minutes.
  3. Filter the purple liquid.
Red or pink = acidic
Purple = neutral
Green or blue = basic

Add a few drops of turmeric solution to the sample.

Yellow stays yellow = acidic or neutral
Yellow turns reddish-brown = basic
Blue litmus turns red = acidic
Red litmus turns blue = basic
No change = neutral

SOCIAL SCIENCE

Social impact

Consumer awareness

Why do companies advertise “pH balanced” products?

Why should consumers read labels before buying toiletries?

Health impact

High-pH products may contribute to skin irritation, while products closer to skin pH are generally more comfortable for regular use.

Environmental impact

Detergents can enter rivers through wastewater, and highly alkaline products may negatively affect aquatic environments.
